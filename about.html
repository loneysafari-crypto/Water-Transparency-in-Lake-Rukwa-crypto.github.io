About the Project

Lake Rukwa Monitor is a cloud-based environmental monitoring platform that uses satellite remote sensing to track water transparency in Lake Rukwa, one of Tanzania's most important wetlands. Built on Google Earth Engine, it turns complex satellite data into clear, accessible insights for researchers, water managers and decision-makers.

Our Mission

Our mission is to develop an efficient, user-friendly system for computing and monitoring water transparency in Lake Rukwa using remote sensing and geospatial technologies — providing accurate, timely, and spatially distributed information that supports environmental monitoring, water resource management, research, and sustainable decision-making.

By integrating Sentinel-2 satellite imagery, geospatial analysis and web mapping technologies, the platform gives users an interactive way to visualize, analyze and track changes in water transparency over time, without the cost and limitations of traditional field-based monitoring.

What We Monitor

The system tracks water transparency, measured as Secchi Disk Depth (ZSD) — a key indicator of aquatic ecosystem health that reflects light penetration, primary productivity and overall water quality. It also captures spatial and seasonal patterns of turbidity and sediment distribution across the lake.

Technology

The platform is powered by Google Earth Engine, processing multi-temporal Sentinel-2 Level-2A Surface Reflectance imagery from year 2017 to 2025. It applies the Msusa22 semi-analytical algorithm, alongside QAA_V5, QAA_TM, QAA_T754 and QAA_T865 models, to classify optical water types and estimate ZSD. Results are delivered through an interactive Web GIS interface built with the Leaflet JavaScript library, requiring no specialized software or technical training to use.

Scientific Background

Water transparency is widely recognized as a key indicator of aquatic ecosystem health because it directly controls light penetration, which in turn affects photosynthesis, primary productivity and overall water quality (Kirk, 2011).

It is commonly measured using Secchi Disk Depth (SDD) or indirectly estimated from turbidity and the optical properties of water. In Lake Rukwa, an endorheic lake located in southwestern Tanzania, recent observations suggest a noticeable decline in water transparency, largely associated with increased sediment inflow, nutrient enrichment, and intensified human activities within the catchment (Mshana, 2015; Lake Rukwa Basin Water Board, 2020).

The deterioration of water transparency is strongly linked to land use and land cover changes, including deforestation, agricultural expansion and small-scale mining activities. These activities increase soil erosion and surface runoff, leading to higher concentrations of suspended sediments entering the lake (FAO, 2015; Wang et al., 2011).

Because Lake Rukwa is endorheic, sediment accumulates within the basin rather than flushing out, making it especially vulnerable to long-term degradation.

Traditional field-based methods such as Secchi disk measurements are labor-intensive and provide limited spatial and temporal coverage. This creates a significant data gap for effective management.

This project addresses these challenges by developing a web-based system that integrates remote sensing data, geospatial analysis and predictive modeling to deliver near real-time information on water transparency in Lake Rukwa, supporting better decision-making and sustainable management of this vital ecosystem.

Key Findings

Analysis of imagery from 2017–2025 shows Lake Rukwa is predominantly composed of Type III and Type IV turbid waters, with ZSD values ranging from 0.060 m to 0.308 m (mean 0.126 m), following consistent seasonal and spatial patterns linked to the lake's sediment-retaining, endorheic nature. These results confirm the platform's value in identifying degraded zones for targeted management action.
